About Connect Four

Connect Four challenges you against the computer to line up four pieces in a row — horizontally, vertically, or diagonally — before your opponent does. Thinking a couple of moves ahead pays off here more than in most casual games.

How to Play Connect Four

Drop pieces to connect 4 in a row — vertical, horizontal or diagonal.

Connect Four Controls

🖱️ Click column

Rules

Standard Connect Four on a 7-column, 6-row board: drop pieces into a column and they fall to the lowest open row. First to connect four of your own in a row -- horizontally, vertically, or diagonally -- wins.

How the AI Plays

Before every move, the computer checks two things: can it win right now by dropping in some column, and if not, can you win next turn in some column it should block instead? If neither applies, it prefers dropping toward the center columns, since central pieces take part in more possible four-in-a-rows than edge ones.

Controls

Click or tap a column to drop your piece (red) into it.

Strategy

Since the AI only checks one move ahead for wins and blocks, it can be beaten with a double threat -- set up two different columns that would each complete four in a row, so blocking one still leaves the other open.

Common Mistakes

Focusing only on your own row or column and missing a diagonal the opponent is quietly building. Playing edge columns early is also weaker, since they participate in fewer possible four-in-a-rows than the center.

History

Vertical four-in-a-row games date back over a century, but the modern version was trademarked and mass-marketed by Milton Bradley in 1974 as "Connect Four," which is where the familiar red-vs-yellow, drop-and-gravity format comes from.

Tips for Connect Four

  • Connect Four tends to reward a steady, controlled pace more than raw speed -- rushing early is usually what ends a run.
  • Watch a full round of Connect Four play out once before trying to optimize your strategy; the patterns get clearer with a little observation.
  • If Connect Four has levels or a difficulty curve, don't skip ahead -- getting the basics solid pays off once things speed up.
