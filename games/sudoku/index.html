Rules

Classic 9×9 Sudoku: fill every row, column, and 3×3 box with the digits 1 through 9, with no repeats in any of the three. Every solvable puzzle here can be worked out through pure logic -- no guessing required.

Puzzle Pool

This implementation ships with a small curated set of easy starter puzzles rather than an endless generator, so you may see a puzzle repeat if you play many rounds back to back.

Checking Your Work

There's no live error-highlighting as you type. You fill in the grid at your own pace and press Check when you're ready, which compares your entire board against the solution at once and tells you whether it's fully correct.

Controls

Click or tap a cell to select it, then click a number 1-9 to fill it in, or the ✕ button to clear it. Your progress saves automatically, so you can close the tab and pick the same puzzle back up later.

Strategy

Scan for a box, row, or column that's missing only one digit first -- those are guaranteed fills. From there, look for cells where only one digit is possible given everything already visible in its row, column, and box.

Common Mistakes

Entering a number without cross-checking it against the row, column, AND box it belongs to -- a placement can look fine in its row while already conflicting in its box. Forgetting that the pre-filled clue cells can't be edited is another common early mix-up.

History

Sudoku's numeral grid traces to Howard Garns's "Number Place," published in the US in 1979. It was popularized in Japan during the 1980s under the name Sudoku ("single number") and became a worldwide puzzle phenomenon in the mid-2000s.

Tips for Sudoku

  • If you're new to the operations in Sudoku, try a few problems on paper first -- speed comes with repetition, not shortcuts.
  • Estimate before you calculate in Sudoku: a rough sense of the answer helps you catch mistakes quickly.
  • A few focused minutes of Sudoku daily builds calculation speed faster than one long, tired session.
