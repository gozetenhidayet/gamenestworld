About Pong

Pong recreates one of the very first video games ever made -- move your paddle to return the ball and score before the computer does. It's minimalist by design and still surprisingly hard to master, especially since the computer gets a slightly higher target to hit: you win first to 5 points, while it takes 7 to beat you.

How to Play Pong

Move your paddle to hit the ball. You win first to 5 points; the computer needs 7 points to win.

Pong Controls

🖱️ Mouse or ↑↓ Arrow keys

Rules & Scoring

First to 5 points wins the match for you -- but the computer needs 7 points to win, a small built-in edge in your favor. Missing the ball scores a point for whoever's side it passed.

Ball Physics

The ball speeds up 5% every time it's hit (capped so it never becomes unplayably fast), and where it strikes your paddle changes its vertical angle -- hitting it near the edge of your paddle sends it off at a steeper angle than a center hit.

How the CPU Plays

The computer paddle simply tracks the ball's vertical position, moving toward it at a capped speed. It doesn't predict trajectory or account for your paddle's angle control, which is the opening a sharper-angled shot exploits.

Controls

Move your mouse to follow the paddle, use the Up/Down arrow keys, or tap the on-screen Up/Down buttons on touch devices.

Strategy

Hit the ball with the edge of your paddle to send it toward a steep angle -- the CPU's speed-capped, position-only tracking can struggle to close the distance in time on a sharp diagonal.

Common Mistakes

Always hitting the ball dead-center returns a predictable, easy-to-track shot straight back at the CPU. Standing still in the middle of your side instead of repositioning after each hit gives up the angle advantage entirely.

History

Pong recreates one of the very first video games ever made, released by Atari in 1972 -- its minimalist ball-and-paddle format is still the reference point the entire genre is named after.

Tips for Pong

  • Pong tends to reward a steady, controlled pace more than raw speed -- rushing early is usually what ends a run.
  • Watch a full round of Pong play out once before trying to optimize your strategy; the patterns get clearer with a little observation.
  • If Pong has levels or a difficulty curve, don't skip ahead -- getting the basics solid pays off once things speed up.

Is Pong's win condition the same for both sides?

No -- you win first to 5 points, while the computer needs 7 to win, a small edge built in on the player's side.

Does the ball get faster over time in Pong?

Yes -- it speeds up slightly with every paddle hit, up to a capped maximum speed so it never becomes unreturnable.
