About Minesweeper

Minesweeper brings back the Windows classic in a compact 6×6 grid — reveal safe cells and use the number clues to figure out where the mines are hiding. Left-click to reveal, right-click to flag a suspected mine.

How to Play Minesweeper

Click to reveal cells — avoid mines! Numbers show nearby mines.

Minesweeper Controls

🖱️ Left click=reveal, Right click=flag

Rules

A 6×6 grid hides 8 mines. Revealing a safe cell shows a number counting its mine-adjacent neighbors (or nothing, if it has none, which auto-reveals the connected empty area around it). Flag any cell you suspect is a mine to keep track of it.

The First Click Isn't Guaranteed Safe

Mines are placed across the board before you click anything, so -- like the original 1990s Minesweeper -- there's no "guaranteed safe" first move built in here. The odds simply favor you early on, since only 8 of the 36 cells are mines.

Controls

Left-click or tap to reveal a cell; right-click to flag a suspected mine (on touchscreens, this generally maps to your browser's long-press context-menu gesture).

Strategy

Use the number clues to deduce, not guess: if a numbered cell already has exactly that many flagged neighbors, every other neighbor of that cell is guaranteed safe to reveal.

Common Mistakes

Clicking a cell you're only 50/50 on when a safer deduction was available elsewhere on the board. Not flagging cells you've already reasoned are mines makes it easy to lose track and misjudge a nearby number later.

History

The version most people know is the one Microsoft bundled with Windows starting in 1990, which popularized the reveal-and-flag, number-clue format this 6×6 mini take is modeled after.
